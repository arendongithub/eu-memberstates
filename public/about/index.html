No union without members

The European Union is a union of peoples and of states. The member state, this foundational building block of the Union, has received only scant attention in comparison to the EU-level institutions and actors that shape its policies.

 

This website was conceived as a complement to the textbook Member States of the European Union (Bloomsbury, forthcoming). In the book, I take a radical focus on the “member state” as an institution in its own right. An institution that might be diverse in its identity but similar in its responsibilities, roles and position within the EU’s structures. This inspired me to withstand the temptation to structure the book along the different member states – devoting chapters to each member state – and instead opt to structure it along cross-cutting dimensions: the process of joining or leaving, how member states formulate positions and defend these at the European level, how national media, civil society or national parliaments engage with the EU.

 

Such a focus, unfortunately, offers little depth or support for those interested in one or two member states. This is what we hope to accomplish through this page. The website aims to provide a stepping stone for students, academics and civil society as they try to unpack how different member states engage with the EU. It can be a source of inspiration to public officials and politicians as they explore alternative ways to engage with the European Union. And it could be a tool to foster greater understanding of fellow member states and help build the road towards a demoi cratic union.

 

I hope it serves you well.

 

Johan

Johan Adriaensen

Associate Professor of European Public Policy
Maastricht University
Johan’s research focuses on European institutional politics and trade policy, with specific attention to the intersection of both topics. His recent works have examined institutional divisions of power in the EU, geoeconomic motives in free trade agreements, and institutional power shifts in the EU’s geoeconomic turn. His extensive expertise in the role of national governments in the field of trade and investment contribute to the external side of statecraft of our project.
