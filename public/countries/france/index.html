France

  • Description
  • Institution Links
France has 68 million inhabitants, representing 15% of the EU’s population. Although it is traditionally a centralised state based in the capital, Paris, it has undergone decentralisation and, since 2016, has consisted of 13 metropolitan and 5 overseas regions. As a semi-presidential republic, its constitution (1958) combines a directly elected president with a prime minister who is accountable to parliament. France was a founding member of the ECSC (1951), the EEC and Euratom (1957). Together with Germany, France has been the driving force behind the integration while also stressing national sovereignty.

 

Accounting for about 17–18% of EU GDP, France is the Union’s second-largest economy and a net contributor to the EU budget. Yet, given the political importance of the agriculture sector, it benefits from the Common Agricultural Policy more than any other state. Around 60% of French trade is with other EU countries France was a founding member of the eurozone in 1999. The economy is dominated by services but retains a strong industrial base in aerospace, defense, automobiles, luxury goods, pharmaceuticals and energy. Its energy position is strengthened by nuclear power.

 

France is the EU’s only permanent member of the UN Security Council and is one of the EU’s “Big Four” states. Its foreign policy is directed to the maintenance of sovereignty and its influence in Africa and the Indo-Pacific. Militarily, France spends around or above NATO’s 2% GDP benchmark, possesses independent nuclear deterrence, a blue-water navy including an aircraft carrier, and full-spectrum military capabilities. France prioritizes European strategic autonomy, a stronger Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP), industrial sovereignty, Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) funding, climate leadership, deeper euro area integration, and a more geopolitical EU capable of acting independently of external powers. To achieve these goals, normally forms coalitions with Germany, Italy, Spain, Belgium, Luxembourg, Portugal and Greece.
